Understanding energy

One meter carries the shape of a whole home.

Your household draws power through one point. Every kettle, fridge, pump and charger passes through the same cable, layered into a single signal that most of us never read.

EnergySense reads that aggregate signal across the day. It learns the rhythm of the house — the quiet baseline, the repeated cycles, the evening surge — and estimates which patterns belong to which kind of appliance.

It does not measure individual appliances directly. There are no extra sensors and no per-device claims; only careful inference, with uncertainty left visible.

“We don’t listen to every appliance. We listen to the house.”

How it works

Three quiet movements.

Nothing about a household is static. EnergySense watches, anticipates, then rehearses a better arrangement of the day.

01 Observe

Analyze energy demand patterns.

The curve is read hour by hour: a slow base load, the short high shoulders of heating and cooking, the long evening climb. Repeated shapes become a vocabulary of use.

Output: an hourly profile and a first map of what the house is doing.

02 Anticipate

Forecast upcoming consumption.

A random-forest model, trained on synthetic smart-meter data, projects the next twenty-four hours of demand — with the household’s own recent rhythm as its strongest prior.

Output: a day-ahead load forecast, and where the costly hours will fall.

03 Adapt

Simulate better schedules.

Flexible loads — a water heater, a washing machine, a charger — are re-timed in simulation. The forecast, tariff and carbon intensity decide the hour; the household decides the boundaries.

Output: a proposed schedule and its modelled cost and carbon difference.

Impact, modelled

A better hour.

The same energy, used later or earlier, can cost less and carry a lighter carbon load. This is what shifting flexible demand looks like across a day.

About

EnergySense is a household energy intelligence study built for an ideathon around SDG 7 — affordable, reliable, and clean energy for all.

A synthetic smart-meter dataset trains the forecast; heuristics read appliance patterns from the aggregate signal; a simulated scheduler proposes a gentler arrangement of the day. The whole system is designed to run locally, offline, on a single machine.

A more thoughtful relationship with energy begins here.
